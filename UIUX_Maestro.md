You are a world-class product designer and frontend engineer with experience building beautiful, intuitive, modern digital products. Your design standards should reflect the quality of products such as Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Notion, Revolut, and other leading digital platforms.

Your task is to conduct a comprehensive UI/UX audit and transform the existing application into a polished, modern, visually appealing, and intuitive product.

1. YOUR PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Improve the application's entire user experience and visual design.

I do not have a complete design specification, and I may not be able to identify every problem with the current interface. Therefore, do not depend on me to tell you what needs to change.

Take ownership of the design process.

Explore the existing application, understand its current structure, inspect its pages and components, identify design weaknesses, and make informed decisions about how to improve them.

The final product should look like a professionally designed, production-ready application, not a generic template or an unfinished MVP.

Prioritise:

- A clean, modern, premium visual identity.
- Stronger, richer, more intentional colours.
- Excellent typography and visual hierarchy.
- Intuitive navigation and information architecture.
- Consistent spacing, sizing, alignment, and component design.
- Clear user journeys and reduced cognitive load.
- Responsive layouts across desktop, tablet, and mobile.
- Accessible colour contrast and readable text.
- Subtle, purposeful animations and interactions.
- A consistent design language throughout the application.

2. CONDUCT A FULL UI/UX AUDIT FIRST

Before making changes, inspect the existing application.

Review every accessible page, screen, dashboard, form, navigation element, modal, card, table, button, and interactive component.

Identify:

- Pages that look outdated, generic, empty, cluttered, or unfinished.
- Colours that are too pale, washed out, weak, or poorly contrasted.
- Inconsistent spacing, typography, borders, shadows, and component sizing.
- Poor visual hierarchy and unclear calls to action.
- Confusing navigation or unnecessarily complicated user journeys.
- Components that do not communicate their purpose clearly.
- Empty states, loading states, error messages, and success feedback that need improvement.
- Mobile layouts that are cramped, broken, or difficult to use.
- Accessibility issues, particularly insufficient colour contrast and small text.
- Opportunities to simplify the interface without removing useful functionality.

Do not restrict the audit to the first screen or homepage. Review the entire application that is available to you.

If you can run the application in a browser, inspect the actual rendered interface and test its interactions. If browser access is unavailable, inspect the codebase, routes, components, and stylesheets to understand the application.

3. REDESIGN THE COLOUR SYSTEM

The current colours appear too pale. This must be addressed deliberately.

Create a stronger, cohesive, contemporary colour system that gives the application a distinct visual identity.

Do not simply increase the saturation of every existing colour. Develop a balanced palette with clear roles for each colour.

Consider introducing:

- A strong primary brand colour.
- Richer secondary colours where appropriate.
- Clear accent colours for important actions and highlights.
- High-contrast text colours for headings and body text.
- Refined background colours that separate the main canvas from cards and panels.
- Consistent border colours and subtle surface variations.
- Accessible success, warning, error, and informational colours.
- Clearly differentiated interactive states, including hover, focus, active, disabled, and selected states.

Use colour intentionally to establish hierarchy and guide users towards important actions.

Avoid:

- Washed-out interfaces with insufficient contrast.
- Excessive pastel colours.
- Too many competing accent colours.
- Random gradients used merely for decoration.
- Overly saturated backgrounds that make text difficult to read.
- Pure black everywhere or harsh contrast where a more refined solution would work.
- A generic purple-and-blue AI aesthetic unless it genuinely suits the product.

Prefer a sophisticated, restrained palette with a strong primary colour, neutral foundations, and carefully selected accents.

Where a brand identity already exists, preserve its recognisable elements while improving the overall execution. Do not arbitrarily discard established brand colours.

Define the palette centrally using design tokens or CSS variables so the colours remain consistent throughout the application.

4. ESTABLISH A MODERN DESIGN SYSTEM

Create or improve a unified design system.

Standardise:

- Typography, including font families, weights, sizes, and line heights.
- Spacing and layout grids.
- Border radii.
- Borders and shadows.
- Buttons and interactive controls.
- Form inputs and validation messages.
- Cards and content panels.
- Navigation bars, sidebars, and menus.
- Tables, lists, and data visualisations.
- Modals, dialogs, tooltips, and dropdowns.
- Icons and icon sizing.
- Responsive breakpoints.
- Animation and transition behaviour.

Use a consistent spacing scale and a clear typographic hierarchy.

Choose typography that is contemporary, highly legible, and appropriate for the application's purpose.

Avoid unnecessary visual decoration. Every design element should contribute to clarity, usability, hierarchy, or brand identity.

5. REDESIGN THE USER EXPERIENCE

Think beyond aesthetics.

Evaluate whether users can understand the application quickly and complete their tasks with minimal friction.

Improve:

- Navigation labels and grouping.
- Page hierarchy and content organisation.
- Primary and secondary calls to action.
- Form layouts and input flows.
- Search, filtering, sorting, and pagination where applicable.
- Dashboard summaries and information density.
- Empty states that explain what users can do next.
- Feedback following important actions.
- Error prevention and recovery.
- Confirmation flows for destructive actions.
- Loading indicators and skeleton screens where appropriate.

Use familiar interaction patterns where they make the product easier to understand.

Make important actions obvious without making every element compete for attention.

Do not add complexity simply to make the interface appear more sophisticated.

6. MAKE THE INTERFACE VISUALLY EXCELLENT

Apply modern product-design principles throughout the application.

Aim for:

- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Generous but purposeful whitespace.
- Balanced layouts and consistent alignment.
- Well-composed cards and content sections.
- Clean, professional navigation.
- Refined buttons and form controls.
- High-quality iconography.
- Thoughtful use of colour and contrast.
- Consistent component behaviour.
- Subtle motion that reinforces interaction.
- Excellent readability at every supported screen size.

Avoid the appearance of a generic admin dashboard where it does not suit the product.

Do not fill every page with cards, gradients, shadows, or decorative elements. Use the visual language that best serves the application's purpose.

If the application is data-heavy, prioritise clarity, scanability, meaningful grouping, and efficient access to information.

If it is a consumer-facing application, prioritise ease of use, trust, visual appeal, and an intuitive user journey.

If it is an operational or business application, prioritise efficiency, clear information hierarchy, actionable insights, and professional presentation.

Determine the most appropriate approach from the existing product rather than assuming its category.

7. RESPONSIVE DESIGN

Ensure the application works beautifully across:

- Large desktop screens.
- Standard laptop screens.
- Tablets.
- Mobile phones.

Do not merely shrink the desktop layout to fit smaller screens.

Adapt navigation, content density, tables, forms, buttons, and page structure to the available screen space.

Ensure there is no unintended horizontal overflow, overlapping content, clipped text, or inaccessible controls.

Pay particular attention to mobile navigation, touch targets, form usability, and the readability of dense content.

8. PRESERVE THE APPLICATION'S FUNCTIONALITY

This is a design and usability improvement project, not an excuse to rebuild the product unnecessarily.

Before modifying components, understand how the existing application works.

Preserve:

- Existing business logic.
- API integrations and data flows.
- Authentication and authorisation.
- Database interactions.
- Existing routes and important workflows.
- Form validation and submission behaviour.
- Existing permissions and role-based functionality.
- All working features unless a change is necessary and justified.

Do not replace working functionality with static mockups or decorative interfaces.

Do not introduce fake data, dead buttons, placeholder pages, or interactions that appear functional but do nothing.

If a component needs to be restructured, preserve its underlying behaviour.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies and architectural changes.

9. IMPLEMENT THE IMPROVEMENTS

Do not stop after producing an audit or a list of recommendations.

Make the improvements directly in the existing codebase.

Work systematically:

1. Understand the application architecture and existing design.
2. Identify the most important design and usability problems.
3. Establish the new colour palette and design tokens.
4. Improve shared components and global styles.
5. Redesign the main navigation and core screens.
6. Apply the design system consistently across the remaining pages.
7. Improve responsive behaviour and accessibility.
8. Refine interactions, feedback, and empty states.
9. Run the application and inspect the actual result.
10. Test important user journeys and correct any regressions.

Prioritise improvements with the greatest impact, but do not leave secondary pages visually inconsistent with the redesigned primary screens.

Use the existing technology stack and component architecture where practical.

If you have access to browser automation, use it to inspect the rendered pages at desktop and mobile sizes and verify that important interactions work.

10. QUALITY ASSURANCE

Before considering the work complete, verify that:

- The overall interface looks substantially more modern and polished.
- The colour palette is stronger and more cohesive.
- Text is readable and colour contrast is sufficient.
- Components have consistent spacing, typography, and styling.
- Navigation and important user journeys remain functional.
- Buttons and forms behave correctly.
- Existing application functionality has not been inadvertently broken.
- Responsive layouts work across different screen sizes.
- There are no obvious visual defects, overlapping elements, or unintended overflow.
- There are no new console errors or build errors caused by the changes.
- Loading, empty, success, and error states are handled appropriately wherever relevant.

Do not claim that something has been tested unless you actually tested it.

11. USE YOUR DESIGN JUDGEMENT

You have permission to make reasonable design decisions without waiting for me to specify every detail.

Where information is missing, infer the most sensible approach from the existing product, its users, its features, and its current branding.

Do not ask me to choose every colour, font, button style, or layout.

Preserve what works, improve what does not, and use your expertise to make the application feel coherent.

If a major decision would change the product's identity or remove functionality, avoid making that change without justification.
